## 1. Side-view posture: plumb line, spinal curves, offsets, pelvic tilt, head/neck carriage

### Takeaway
The textbook (Kendall) side plumb line runs ear canal – mid-shoulder – slightly behind the hip-joint centre (greater trochanter) – slightly in front of the knee axis – slightly in front of the lateral malleolus, but it is a convention with no documented measurement behind it; measured data say the real line of gravity falls about 5 cm in front of the ankle joint and roughly through the hip joint. A realistic side profile needs four things a stack of vertical rings lacks: a pelvis tipped forward about 8–13°, a lumbar hollow that sits about 4 cm in front of the rearmost point of the upper back, a ribcage that leans back, and a neck that leans forward so the ear sits well in front of the base of the neck (ear–C7 line is about 50° above horizontal).

### Cited Findings

**The plumb line (ideal alignment)**
- [clinical convention] Kendall's lateral ideal alignment: "The plumb line passes through the external auditory canal, midway through the shoulder, slightly posterior to centre of hip joint, slightly anterior to axis of knee joint, and slightly anterior to the lateral malleolus of the ankle." — [Journal of Rehabilitation Medicine 2024 scoping review, PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11492508)
- [clinical convention] Teaching handouts add: through the lobe of the ear, the shoulder joint, the bodies of the lumbar (and cervical) vertebrae, and the greater trochanter; one handout ends the line at the calcaneocuboid joint. — [SlideShare posture lecture](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/posture-257822035/257822035); [Physio-pedia Posture](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Posture?amp=1); [Scribd posture evaluation handout](https://www.scribd.com/doc/178731525/PostureEvaluation); [Brainscape posture flashcards](https://www.brainscape.com/flashcards/posture-assessment-5716060/packs/8695364); [Scribd "The Standing Posture"](https://www.scribd.com/document/51041206/The-Standing-Posture)
- [clinical convention] The plumb bob is hung just anterior to the lateral malleolus (this is the fixed reference point for the test). — [ACSM, Assessing Static Posture](https://acsm.org/assessing-static-posture-1/)
- [clinical convention] The line "falls slightly anterior to the lateral malleolus and the midline of the knee joint when the knee is fully extended, slightly posterior to the hip joint axis when the hip is fully extended." — [TESU FIT-211 Kinesiology posture screening](https://www2.tesu.edu/dial/fit/fit211/modules_fit-211-jan12/Posture_Screening_FIT-211-nov11/Posture_Screening_FIT211nov11.html)
- [anatomy text] The line of gravity passes behind the hip joints and in front of the knee and ankle joints; body weight therefore tends to hyperextend the hip, resisted by the iliofemoral ligament. — [Dartmouth Basic Human Anatomy, ch. 18](https://humananatomy.host.dartmouth.edu/BHA/public_html/part_3/chapter_18.html)
- **Caveat:** "No edition of Kendall's manual provides a bibliographic reference or details of the procedure used to establish these reference points"; the 2024 review ("The standard posture is a myth") concludes the "ideal alignment" does not match the actual line of gravity or comfortable posture. — [PMC11492508](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11492508); [JRM article page](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/jrm/article/view/41899)
- Physio-pedia notes there is no universally accepted definition of ideal posture and that current thinking favours individual variation. — [Physio-pedia Posture](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Posture?amp=1)

**Measured position of the line of gravity**
- [measured] Quiet-standing study (Di Giulio & Baltzopoulos, Frontiers in Physiology 2019): centre-of-gravity line was 47 mm (mid-trial) to 58 mm (end of trial) anterior of the ankle joint, and 2 mm to −5 mm from the hip joint (i.e. essentially through the hip joint). Sign convention for the hip value was not visible in the extract; no knee distance was retrieved. — [Frontiers in Physiology 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2019.00625/full)

**Spinal curve angles (radiographic Cobb angles, standing)**
- [measured] Asymptomatic adults on standing lateral radiographs: lumbar lordosis mean about 48.8° in men (range 27–78°, SD 9.8, n=101) and about 50.5° in women; thoracic kyphosis mean about 32.6° in men (range 10–59°, SD 10.9) and about 33.9° in women. *(attribution per search extract)* — [BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders 2022, table 1](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12891-022-05379-6/tables/1)
- [reference ranges] Commonly quoted normal ranges: thoracic kyphosis 20–40° and lumbar lordosis 40–60°, cervical lordosis 20–40° — [NY Spine](https://www.nyspine.com/blog/what-are-the-four-normal-curves-of-the-spine/); thoracic kyphosis Cobb 20–50° — [Radiopaedia case](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/67650/studies/77873?lang=us); AO Foundation gives single typical values of about 40° thoracic (T1–T12), about 60° lumbar and about 15° cervical (C2–C7) *(values as given in the search extract)* — [AO Surgery Reference, sagittal spinal alignment](https://surgeryreference.aofoundation.org/spine/tumors/primary-tumors/further-reading/sagittal-spinal-alignment)
- [measured] Thoracic kyphosis increases with age, faster in women (316 normal subjects). — [Fon et al., AJR 1980, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6768276/)
- Conflict: a review found that "for the cervical region, it was impossible to establish consistent reference values", and a separate EOS-imaging study of healthy 18–60-year-olds reported quite different means (lordosis 32.4 ± 6.3°, kyphosis 43.6 ± 6.4°) — method and population change the numbers a lot. *(attribution per search extract; one of the PMC articles returned)* — [PMC7221279](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7221279); [PMC7280923](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7280923)

**Front/back surface offsets of the back profile (cm)**
- [measured] Stagnara "plumb-line distances": with a plumb line held tangent to the most posterior point (apex) of the thoracic kyphosis, the horizontal gap from the line to the skin was 39.9 ± 16.7 mm at C7, 21.4 ± 15.3 mm at T12, 39.9 ± 15.0 mm at L3 and 20.6 ± 17.0 mm at S2 (584 healthy individuals). Sagittal index (C7 + L3) = 79.8 ± 26.8 mm; ≥ 95 mm was specific for hyperkyphosis ≥ 60°. Limitation: the sample was mostly children/adolescents, not adults. — [Physical Therapy 2019, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31504925/); [full text, Univ. Milan repository](https://air.unimi.it/bitstream/2434/776981/2/Clinical%20Evaluation%20of%20Spinal%20Coronal%20and%20Sagittal%20Balance%20in%20584%20Healthy%20Individuals.pdf)
- [measured, radiographic] In a balanced spine the vertical dropped from the centre of C7 falls within about 3 cm of the posterior-superior corner of the sacrum (S1); i.e. the base of the neck sits vertically above the back of the sacrum. — [StatPearls, sagittal balance](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/41489)

**Pelvic tilt**
- [measured] Angle of the ASIS–PSIS line to horizontal (anterior tilt) in asymptomatic adults: radiographic mean 13 ± 6°, range −4.5° to 27° (Vialle); men 9.6 ± 3.5° and 11.7 ± 3.8° in two groups (McKeon & Hertel); healthy female students 12.2 ± 5.2° (right) and 11.8 ± 4.5° (left). — [IJSPT, Clinical measures of pelvic tilt](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/27978)
- [measured] "The normal neutral pelvic angle in standing appears to be sex dependent, with mean values of approximately 9° for males and 12° for females"; the same Korean study measured men at 8.4 ± 5.2° and found the sex difference not significant in its own sample. — [KoreaScience paper](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO202014862252019.pdf)
- [measured] On dry bone the ASIS–PSIS angle averaged 13° (SD 5), range 0–23°, so part of "tilt" is just pelvic shape. *(attribution per search extract)* — [PMC2565125](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2565125)
- A systematic review gives about 8° as the reference anterior tilt of the healthy population. *(attribution per search extract)* — [PMC8486407](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8486407)

**Head and neck carriage**
- [measured] Craniovertebral angle (CVA: angle between horizontal and the line from the C7 spinous process to the tragus of the ear) in 122 healthy adults: mean 48.76°; women 50.07°, men 47.46°; falls about 1.6° per decade of age. — [Applied Sciences 2024 (MDPI)](https://mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/19/8639)
- [measured] Standing CVA of 98 young adults: 50.0 ± 5.2° (sitting is lower). — [Int J Exerc Sci conference abstract](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/ijesab/vol16/iss3/53)
- [artistic convention] "The basic form of the neck is a cylinder. The axis of this cylinder is tilted forward to counterbalance the angle of rib cage"; "the neck is lower in the front and higher in the back." — [Proko, How to Draw Neck Muscles – Form (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-neck-muscles-form/notes)
- [artistic convention] The head is "held out over the rib cage by the forward angle of the neck", with the ribcage tilting the opposite way to balance it; the pelvis in turn tilts opposite to the ribcage. — [Michael Hampton, Figure Drawing: Design and Invention (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/download/michael-hampton-figure-drawing-design-an/Michael_Hampton_Figure_Drawing_Design_an_text.pdf)
- [artistic convention] In side view the whole body reads as a large "S"; "C" and "S" curves are the key lines of a figure. *(attribution per search extract)* — [Bridgman's Complete Guide to Drawing from Life (archive.org text)](https://ia600707.us.archive.org/24/items/BridgmansCompleteGuideToDrawingFromLife_201812/Bridgman%27s%20Complete%20Guide%20To%20Drawing%20From%20Life_text.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Recommended side-view skeleton for the mannequin (derived from the findings above, not a quoted table).** Take a vertical reference line through the ear canal and mid-shoulder. Place the hip joint / greater trochanter on that line (measured: within ±0.5 cm), the knee joint centre a little behind it ("slightly anterior to the knee axis" — no cm value was sourced; 1–2 cm is a guess), and the ankle joint / lateral malleolus behind it. Here convention and measurement disagree: Kendall puts the line only "slightly" in front of the lateral malleolus, whereas the measured line of gravity was 47–58 mm in front of the ankle joint; 3–5 cm is a defensible default. Either way the leg as a whole leans slightly *forward* from ankle to hip, and the foot projects forward under the line.
- **Back profile:** if the most rearward point of the upper back is at depth 0, the base of the neck (C7) should be about 4 cm further forward, the thoraco-lumbar junction about 2 cm forward, the deepest lumbar hollow (L3) about 4 cm forward and the sacrum about 2 cm forward — so upper back and sacrum are close to the same vertical, with a 4 cm hollow between and the neck base set 4 cm in. The buttock mass then projects behind the sacrum (amount not sourced). Adult values may be somewhat larger than these adolescent means.
- **Angles:** tilt the pelvis ring(s) forward about 10° (men about 9°, women about 12°); give the lumbar segment about 40–60° of total lordosis and the thoracic segment about 20–40° (mean about 33°) of kyphosis. In practice this means the ribcage "egg" leans *backward* at the top and the pelvis block leans *forward* at the top, the two opposing each other.
- **Head forward of the trunk:** a CVA of about 50° means the ear canal lies on a line rising at 50° from horizontal from the C7 bump — i.e. inclined 40° forward of vertical. For every 10 cm the ear is above C7 it is about 8.4 cm in front of it (tan 40°). The neck *axis* is less inclined than this (C7 is the back surface of the neck base, the ear is mid-head), but a vertical neck with the head centred over it is clearly wrong.
- Because the plumb line itself is a convention and normal people vary by several cm and ±5–10°, these numbers should be defaults with sliders, not constants.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved gave cm offsets of the *front* surface landmarks (chest/sternum, abdomen, front of thigh) from the plumb line, nor buttock protrusion behind the sacrum, nor occiput position; the only surface offsets found are the back-profile distances above (adolescent sample).
- No measured knee-axis-to-gravity-line distance was retrieved (only "slightly anterior").
- No source gave a neck-axis inclination in degrees; only the CVA (ear–C7 line) and the qualitative "tilted forward". Background knowledge (unverified this session): artists typically draw the neck axis leaning forward roughly 15–25° from vertical.
- Kendall's own book could not be opened; the wording is from secondary sources.

## 2. Torso forms

### Takeaway
The trunk is not one tapered barrel but two opposed masses — an egg-shaped ribcage (narrow at the top, widest low down, about 1.5 heads tall × 1.25 wide × 1 deep) and a pelvis block — separated by a soft, narrower waist about a hand's width tall; shoulders slope about 20° down from the neck, and the arm is tied to the trunk by two armpit folds (pectoral in front, latissimus behind, the rear one lower). Sex differences are mainly widths: male shoulders about 40 cm vs female about 36 cm (bone-to-bone), female pelvis wider and shorter so that it is wider than her ribcage, and the female breast occupies ribs 2–6 with the nipple at about the 4th intercostal space.

### Cited Findings

**Ribcage, waist, pelvis as masses**
- [artistic convention] "The rib cage is more like an egg because the top is narrower than the bottom. And more specifically, the rib cage is an egg with planes." — [Proko, Anatomy of the Rib Cage (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/anatomy-of-the-rib-cage/notes)
- [artistic convention] Ribcage size rule quoted on Proko's rib cage page: "Ribcage is 1.5 heads tall, 1.25 heads wide, 1 head deep." — [Proko, Anatomy of the Rib Cage](https://proko.com/anatomy-of-the-rib-cage-for-artists)
- [artistic convention] In Hale's cranial units: "The ribcage is about 2 craniums tall, and the pelvis is 1 + a half." — [Proko, Human Proportions – Cranial Units (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-cranial-units/notes)
- [artistic convention] The space between ribcage and pelvis "is nearly about the width of the person's hand"; the torso is about 2 heads wide from front/back and 1 head wide in profile (instructor replies/lesson text on Proko; exact page not confirmed). — [Proko, Robo Bean (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-structure-in-the-body-robo-bean/notes); [Proko community thread](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/please-can-anyone-critique)
- [low-authority tutorial] The widest part of the ribcage is around the 7th–8th rib. — [Autodesk "rib cage drawing" page](https://origin-exchange.autodesk.com/rib-cage-drawing)
- [artistic convention] The pelvis can be simplified as half an egg or a box; "the angle of the pelvis is normally in opposition of the angle of the rib cage"; even standing straight there is "a slight forward tilt" of the pelvis. — [Royal Drawing School teaching resource](https://royaldrawingschool.org/living-library/teaching-resource-the-point-of-perspective); [Proko community thread](https://www.proko.com/community/topics/please-can-anyone-critique)
- [artistic convention] Proko's mannequinization simplifies the torso into two boxes (ribcage and pelvis) with a flexible middle ("bean" / "robo bean"). — [Proko, Mannequinization](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/mannequinization-structure-of-the-human-body)

**Shoulder line, trapezius slope, clavicles**
- [apparel measurement] Shoulder slope (angle between the line neck-root → shoulder end and the horizontal) is typically 19° for women and 21° for men (Liu Ruipu's pattern text); measured range in 64 subjects 16.1°–32.8°; "standard" female type 21–23°. — [Journal of Fiber Bioengineering and Informatics review](https://ojs.global-sci.org/index.php/JFBI/article/download/13167/26249/27479)
- [apparel standard] ASTM sizing table lists shoulder slope from 19.875° (size 00P) down to 17.25° (size 20P). — [ASTM D7878](https://www.astm.org/d7878_d7878m-13.html)
- [tailoring convention, different definition] A clothing-fit patent calls 25° the "normal" shoulder. — [US patent 7584122](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/7584122)
- [measured] Biacromial (bony shoulder) breadth, US adults 19–65, 5th/50th/95th percentile: men 365/400/435 mm; women 330/360/390 mm. — [CityU Online Anthropometry, ch. 2-18](https://personal.cityu.edu.hk/meachan/Online%20Anthropometry/Chapter2/Ch2-18.htm)
- [artistic convention] Male clavicles are drawn longer and angled, female shorter and more horizontal; male figure a trapezoid tapering from wide shoulders to narrow hips, female an hourglass. *(attribution per search extract)* — [MediBang, differences between male and female skeletons](https://medibangpaint.com/en/use/2021/03/differences-between-male-and-female-skeletons); [Fiveable drawing foundations](https://fiveable.me/key-terms/drawing-foundations/male-vs-female-differences)
- [artistic convention] The trapezius is "a very large, flattish muscle that encompasses parts of the neck, shoulder and back." — [Creative Bloq, how to draw neck and shoulders](https://www.creativebloq.com/how-to/draw-neck-and-shoulders)

**Chest, armpit, latissimus**
- [artistic convention] Pectoralis major is "structured like a folding fan", bundles radiating from the armpit; the sternal portion converges toward the armpit and dips under the clavicular portion. — [Proko, How to Draw Pecs – Anatomy & Form (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-pecs-anatomy-and-form/notes)
- [anatomy] The anterior axillary fold is formed by pectoralis major, the posterior fold by latissimus dorsi and teres major; the hollow between is the axillary fossa; the upper limb attaches to the thorax through these two folds. — [WSU gross anatomy text, pectoral region](https://learning.medicine.wsu.edu/gross-anatomy/textbook/8-pectoral)
- [artistic convention] The rear wall of the armpit is deeper/longer than the front wall because the latissimus attaches farther down the back, and rounder because its fibres turn on themselves before reaching the arm bone. — [Chest of Books, "The Armpit" (classic artistic anatomy)](https://chestofbooks.com/arts/drawing/Anatomy/The-Armpit-The-Neck-Movements-Of-The-Neck.html)

**Back: shoulder blades**
- [anatomy] At rest the medial border of the scapula is about 5 cm from the vertebral column (varies with protraction/retraction). — [StatPearls/NCBI Bookshelf NBK531475](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK531475/) Contradicted by a palpation reference giving 3–4 cm lateral to the thoracic spinous processes. — [GPnotebook, borders of scapula](https://gpnotebook.com/pages/musculoskeletal-medicine/bony-palpation/borders-of-scapula-examination)
- [anatomy] The scapula spans from about the 2nd rib (medial border begins level with the T3 spinous process) to the inferior angle at about T7 / 7th rib (some say T7–T8); posture alters these levels. — [GPnotebook](https://gpnotebook.com/pages/musculoskeletal-medicine/bony-palpation/borders-of-scapula-examination); [PMC2647093](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2647093)

**Pelvis width, hips, buttocks (male vs female)**
- [artistic convention] "A female pelvis is about 2 finger widths wider and 2 finger widths shorter than a male pelvis." — [Proko, Anatomy of the Pelvis (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/anatomy-of-the-pelvis/notes)
- [artistic convention] "Her pelvis is wider than her rib cage. The male pelvis is narrow, deep and approximately the same width as his rib cage." — [Drafting manual, figure drawing (tpub)](https://draftingmanuals.tpub.com/14263/css/Figure-Drawing-Cont-144.htm)
- [artistic convention] The female pelvis is tilted further forward than the male (male described as "tilted backwards"). *(attribution per search extract)* — [MediBang](https://medibangpaint.com/en/use/2021/03/differences-between-male-and-female-skeletons)
- [artistic convention] Richer average figure: female hips 1.5 heads between the two greater trochanters, male slightly narrower; Loomis keeps hips at 1.5 heads and widens the (male) shoulders to 2⅓ heads. — [Proko, Human Proportions – Average Figure (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-average-figure/notes); [Proko, Idealistic Figures (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-idealistic-figures/notes)
- [artistic convention] The greater trochanter is "a prominent bony landmark, which is also the widest part of the hips." — [Anatomy for Sculptors blog, Butt bones and body proportions](https://anatomy4sculptors.com/blog/butt-bones-and-body-proportions/)
- [anatomy] The greater trochanter's top sits about 2–4 cm lower than the femoral head; the wider female pelvic outlet gives a greater distance between the trochanters. — [Wikipedia, Greater trochanter](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_trochanter)
- [measured] Bi-iliac (iliac crest) breadth, US men 20+: mean 29.6 cm (28.5 cm at age 20–29). — [NHANES III table 57 (archived CDC)](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081024180411/http://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhanes/t57.pdf) A small study (17 men, 17 women) gave 27.0 (SD 2.0) cm men, 25.5 (SD 1.6) cm women — bony crest width is not larger in women in absolute terms in that sample. *(attribution per search extract)* — [PMC3011859 table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3011859/table/T1)
- [fashion-drawing convention] "In women, the hips are often just a little wider than shoulder width"; a man's hips are narrower than his shoulders. — [Fashion Drawing For Dummies, waists and hips](https://dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/art-architecture/drawing/fashion-drawing/how-to-draw-waists-and-hips-on-fashion-figures-171547)
- [artistic convention] The buttock muscles "wrap around the greater trochanter, which is right in the center of the hips"; women carry a thicker fat layer that softens the hips ("even on someone very skinny, there's a layer of fat hiding the muscle definition"); the two buttocks plus gluteus medius read as a "butterfly" shape; gluteus medius fills (sometimes over-fills, slightly convex) the area between crest and trochanter. — [Proko, How to Draw Butts (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-butts/notes)

**Female bust**
- [anatomy] The breast base extends vertically from the 2nd to the 6th rib and horizontally from the lateral border of the sternum to the mid-axillary line; with age it can descend below the 6th rib. — [Elsevier Complete Anatomy, breast](https://www.elsevier.com/resources/anatomy/integument/breast-left/breast-left/21685); [StatPearls, breast anatomy](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/18562)
- [anatomy] The nipple lies at about the 4th intercostal space / 4th rib, just lateral to the midclavicular line, and in young women just above the inframammary crease. — [UCLA Health, Breast Anatomy](https://www.uclahealth.org/radiology/breast-anatomy); [StatPearls](https://www.statpearls.com/point-of-care/18562)
- [aesthetic-surgery measurement] Preferred profile: 45% of breast height above the nipple (upper pole) and 55% below (Mallucci & Branford). — [American Society of Plastic Surgeons press release](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/press-releases/plastic-surgery-researchers-ask-whats-the-perfect-breast) Contradicted in part by a Swedish survey of 2,000 people preferring 50:50. *(attribution per search extract)* — [Aesthetic Plastic Surgery 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00266-020-01777-0)
- [aesthetic-surgery measurement] A 109-volunteer study reported an "ideal" sternal-notch-to-nipple distance of 21–21.5 cm. — [Liu & Thomson 2011, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21301308/)

### Inferences
- **Replace the single stack of ellipses with two masses plus a bridge.** Ribcage: an egg, top narrow at the neck base, widest at roughly 2/3–3/4 of the way down (ribs 7–8), about 1.5 head-heights tall, 1.25 wide and 1 deep, leaning back at the top. Pelvis: a block about 1.5 heads wide at the trochanters, leaning forward at the top about 10°. Between them a waist about one hand-width (roughly 8–10 cm; hand-width figure itself not sourced in cm) tall whose rings are narrower than both — this is what stops the "tube" look. The abdomen's forward bulge follows from the lumbar lordosis plus forward pelvic tilt: the front of the belly between ribcage and pubis should be slightly convex, not a straight chord.
- **Shoulder slope:** with a 20° slope and biacromial 40 cm (m) / 36 cm (f), the acromion sits several cm below the neck root (drop = horizontal run × tan 20° ≈ 0.36 × run). The shoulder line should not be horizontal.
- **Shoulder width vs bone width:** biacromial 40 cm is bone-to-bone; the deltoids add to this on each side, which is how the artistic "2 to 2⅓ heads" overall shoulder width (about 45–52 cm for a 22–23 cm head) is reached.
- **Male V-taper vs female hourglass:** men — shoulder mass widest, ribcage about as wide as pelvis, latissimus fills the gap from the rear armpit down toward the waist so the back outline runs in a straight diagonal from armpit to waist. Women — ribcage narrower than the pelvis; trochanter-level width about equal to or slightly more than shoulder width; fat pad makes the hip outline a continuous curve peaking at or slightly below the trochanter.
- **Armpit:** model two folds, not a butt joint: a front web from chest to arm ending higher, and a rear web (latissimus/teres) ending lower and thicker.
- **Back:** two shallow raised plates (scapulae) from about T2/T3 to T7, their inner edges 3–5 cm either side of a midline furrow; the furrow deepens in the lumbar region.
- **Bust:** a teardrop mass whose footprint runs from rib 2 to rib 6 and from the sternal edge to the side of the chest, apex slightly outside the mid-clavicular line, 45% of its height above the apex and 55% below; it merges into the chest gradually above (no step) and has a defined fold below. On an 8-head figure the apex sits near the 2-heads-down line (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No source retrieved gave the exact level where the ribcage ends relative to stature (10th rib / L2–L3), the navel–iliac-crest relationship, or the vertebral level of the iliac crest (commonly taught as L4) and the sacral dimples (PSIS, S2) — the searches did not return them. Unverified background knowledge: the lowest ribs at the side are about at elbow level, the natural waist lies between them and the iliac crest, and the navel is at or just above crest level.
- Nothing retrieved quantified the gluteal fold height or the buttock's rearward projection; nothing on the spine-furrow depth.

## 3. Shoulder and arm

### Takeaway
The arm is not a straight tube: the deltoid caps the shoulder and inserts about halfway down the outside of the humerus; the upper arm is deeper front-to-back than it is wide, while the forearm is the opposite — fullest in its upper half just below the elbow, tapering to a wrist that is flat and wider side-to-side. At rest the arm hangs with the elbow bent about 15° and the forearm angled outward from the upper arm by a carrying angle of roughly 10° in men and 13–15° in women.

### Cited Findings
- [anatomy] The deltoid's fibres from clavicle, acromion and scapular spine converge to one tendon on the deltoid tuberosity, "approximately halfway down the lateral aspect of the shaft of the humerus" (middle third of the anterolateral surface). — [Wikipedia, Deltoid tuberosity](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deltoid_tuberosity); [Elsevier Complete Anatomy, deltoid tuberosity](https://www.elsevier.com/resources/anatomy/skeletal-system/appendicular-skeleton/deltoid-tuberosity/19668); [Kenhub, deltoid](https://kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/the-deltoid-muscle)
- [measured, cadaveric] The deltoid insertion is long: mean length 97 mm (range 83–111 mm). — [Orthobullets evidence summary](https://www.orthobullets.com/evidence/16424805)
- [artistic convention] "The upper arm is wider from the side view than from the front view"; the forearm is the opposite of the upper arm in its width-to-depth relationship, connecting "in a much flatter and wider way" into the palm and back of the hand. — [Sinix "Anatomy Quick Tips: Arms" transcript](https://production-blue.amara.org/subtitles/1M24UT8ZjT2f/en/2/download/Anatomy%20Quick%20Tips%20Arms.en.dfxp)
- [artistic convention] The forearm is like "a chicken drumstick": thick and meaty in the top half, mostly bone and tendon in the bottom half; the extensor group is a bullet shape with its tip at the dimple of the lateral epicondyle. — [Proko, How to Draw Forearms (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-forearms-anatomy-for-artists/notes)
- [artistic convention] "The upper portion of the forearm is fuller and rounder, while the wrist is slimmer and more squared-off where the muscles have become thin and tendinous." — [Ephraim Rubenstein, Drawing the Arm (PDF)](https://ephraimrubenstein.com/wp-content/uploads/DrawingArmRotOCR.compressed.pdf)
- [artistic convention] The radius is wider at the wrist end. — [Proko, How to Draw Arm Bones (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-arm-bones-anatomy-for-artists/notes)
- [measured] Standing neutral posture in upright CT (palms resting on the sides of the thighs): elbow median 15.5° flexion (IQR 13.2–18.1°), 9.8° valgus (IQR 6.9–12.4°), forearm 90.2° pronation (IQR 79.6–99.4°). — [J Orthop Surg Res 2022](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13018-022-03113-5.pdf)
- [measured] Carrying angle by sex: commonly cited 10–15° men, 15–20° women (review of earlier literature) — [PMC3355210](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3355210) *(attribution per search extract)*; Central Indian adults (n=300): men about 9.6–10.8°, women about 13.3–13.7°; Saudi women aged 18–24 (n=165): 13.2 ± 1.8° right, 13.0 ± 1.7° left — [PMC12156686](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12156686) *(attribution per search extract)*; Indian students: 13.6° women vs 6.7° men — [Int J Med Biomed Res (AJOL)](https://ajol.info/index.php/ijmbr/article/view/99329/88623)
- Contradicting evidence: a radiographic study of 422 patients found the carrying angle increased with age "but there were no real differences in males and females." — [Beals 1976, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/954311/)
- [artistic convention] Hanging-arm landmarks: elbows about level with the waist/navel (one source: bottom of the ribcage; tuts+ places them on "level 3" of an 8-head figure); wrists about level with the crotch / greater trochanter; fingertips reach mid-thigh or "slightly below the middle of the thigh"; the arm is about 3 heads from armpit to middle fingertip. — [Fiveable, essential human figure proportions](https://fiveable.me/lists/essential-human-figure-proportions); [Science Insights, proportioning a body](https://scienceinsights.org/how-to-proportion-a-body-for-figure-drawing/); [Tuts+ Human Anatomy Fundamentals: Basic Body Proportions](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/human-anatomy-fundamentals-basic-body-proportions--vector-18254); [Making Comics, standard proportions](https://makingcomics.com/2014/01/19/standard-proportions-human-body/)
- [modelling critique] A common base-mesh error flagged by reviewers: "the calves and the forearms are rather large for a realistic human." — [Blender Artists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/anatomy-proportions-need-someone-to-check-dummy-model-for-errors/675887)

### Inferences
- **Cross-sections to use instead of circles:** upper arm = ellipse with the long axis front-to-back (biceps in front, triceps behind); forearm just below the elbow = fuller ellipse with the long axis roughly side-to-side when the palm faces forward (the flexor/extensor masses sit either side); wrist = flat ellipse, clearly wider than thick. Because the hanging forearm is pronated about 90° (palm to thigh), the wrist's flat face is turned toward the thigh, so from the front the hanging wrist looks narrow and from the side it looks wide — the opposite of the upper arm above it. The ellipse axes should therefore twist about 90° between elbow and wrist.
- **Taper profile:** widest at the deltoid (about one-third of the way down the upper arm), a slight pinch where the deltoid inserts at mid-humerus, a second swell just below the elbow, then a steady taper to the wrist (upper half of the forearm meaty, lower half bone and tendon).
- **Deltoid cap:** an inverted teardrop from the acromion/clavicle down to a point at about 50% of humerus length on the outer side; with an insertion nearly 10 cm long, its lower tip can reach slightly past mid-humerus. It should overhang the shoulder joint laterally so the shoulder's widest point is below the acromion, not at it.
- **Pose defaults:** elbow flexed about 15°, forearm deviating about 10° (male) or about 13–15° (female) outward from the humerus line in the frontal plane, forearm pronated so the palm faces the thigh. The sex difference in carrying angle is real in most studies but small and disputed; a 3–5° difference is defensible.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved gave numeric cross-section ratios (depth:width) for the upper arm, forearm or wrist; the direction of the asymmetry is sourced, the magnitude is not.
- The statement "forearm widest just below the elbow" is supported only as "upper half fuller/meaty"; no source gave the level as a fraction of forearm length.
- The Proko deltoid lesson (teardrop shape, exact insertion wording) could not be opened.
- A conflicting "neutral elbow angle of 107° ± 10°" from an elbow-stiffness study uses a different angle convention/setup and was not reconciled. — [PubMed 19643518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19643518/)

## 4. Leg

### Takeaway
The leg is a sequence of alternating, asymmetric swellings: thighs converge from wide hips to knees set closer together (femur and tibia shafts meet at about 5–7° of valgus; Q angle about 13–14° in men, 16–17° in women), the thigh is fullest on the outside high up and on the inside low down (teardrop above the kneecap), the calf is "high on the outside, low and heavy on the inside", and the ankle reverses that — inner ankle bone higher and further forward, outer about 1 cm lower and further back. In side view the front of the shin is nearly straight while the calf bulges behind, high on the lower leg.

### Cited Findings

**Alignment: knees closer than hips**
- [measured] The anatomical axes of the femoral and tibial shafts form a lateral angle of 173–175°, i.e. 5–7° of physiological valgus at the knee; < 5° is classed varus and > 7° valgus in one study; the mechanical hip–knee–ankle line is close to 180° (straight). — [Wikipedia, Femoral-tibial angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femoral-tibial_angle); [Thieme journal article](https://thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/html/10.1055/s-0043-1770085) *(attribution per search extract)*
- [anatomy] In women the femora converge more than in men because the pelvis is wider. — [Wikipedia, Femoral-tibial angle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femoral-tibial_angle)
- [measured] Q angle (ASIS → patella centre → tibial tubercle): 269 men / 257 women — standing 13.6° men, 17.0° women (supine 12.7° / 15.8°). — [PubMed 1558251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1558251) A later normative study: men 13.5°, women 15.9°, 95% limits about ±5°. *(attribution per search extract)* — [PMC6564690](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6564690)
- Contradicting evidence: men and women differed by only 2.3° on average and "men and women of equal height demonstrated similar Q angles". — [Grelsamer et al. 2005, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16260666/)
- [measured, gait] Foot progression (toe-out) angle: −3° to +20° is accepted as normal. *(attribution per search extract)* — [PMC4886808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4886808)

**Thigh**
- [artistic convention] Vastus medialis is "a very prominent teardrop shape on the inside just above the patella" and is the lowest of the quadriceps; in profile vastus lateralis "looks like a large football high on the leg", from the greater trochanter to just above the patella; "the side of the thigh is defined by the vastus lateralis and is usually fairly flat"; "the rectus femoris bulges out in the middle"; sartorius and tensor fasciae latae make a V at the top leading into the big teardrop of the quads; the cross-section is like a slice of bread with the two vasti forming the rounded top. — [Proko, How to Draw Legs – The Quad Muscles (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-legs-the-quad-muscles/notes); [Japanese mirror of the notes](https://w.atwiki.jp/proko_translation/pages/72.html)
- [artistic convention] The leg tapers "supplely from the thigh to the ankle", each segment also tapering on its own, giving an undulating rhythm of swelling and narrowing. — [Ephraim Rubenstein, Drawing the Leg (PDF)](https://ephraimrubenstein.com/wp-content/uploads/DrawingLegRotOCR.pdf)
- [artistic convention] Bridgman's shorthand: round thigh, square knee, triangular calf, square ankle. *(attribution per search extract)* — [Creative Bloq, how to draw legs](https://www.creativebloq.com/how-to/draw-legs); [Bridgman's Complete Guide (archive.org text)](https://ia600707.us.archive.org/24/items/BridgmansCompleteGuideToDrawingFromLife_201812/Bridgman%27s%20Complete%20Guide%20To%20Drawing%20From%20Life_text.pdf)

**Lower leg and calf**
- [artistic convention] Calf silhouette is "slanted, high on the outside, low and heavy on the inside"; the lateral head of gastrocnemius "ends higher and tends to create an oblique edge that flows into the soleus". — [Proko, How to Draw the Calf (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-calf-anatomy-for-artists/notes)
- [artistic convention] From the inside profile "gastrocnemius is big and round, while the soleus is wide and flat"; soleus "pokes out from underneath the edges of gastrocnemius, especially at the ankle where the soleus muscle belly descends lower and is wider"; "roughly 2/3 down the leg, soleus begins to narrow and interlace with the Achilles tendon"; overall form "an upside down bowling pin... or a chicken drumstick". — [Proko, How to Draw the Calf (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-calf-anatomy-for-artists/notes)
- [anatomy] The medial head of gastrocnemius is generally thicker and wider than the lateral head. *(attribution per search extract)* — [Radiopaedia, gastrocnemius](https://radiopaedia.org/articles/6024); [Physio-pedia, Gastrocnemius](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Gastrocnemius?lang=en)
- [measured, cadaveric] Muscle–tendon junction of the medial head at a median 44% (33–52%) and of the lateral head at 53% (39–62%) of fibular length. The extract says distances were normalised from the lateral malleolus; if so the medial belly ends lower than the lateral, agreeing with Proko, but the measurement direction was not verified. — [PubMed 27292288](https://www.pubmed.gov/27292288/)
- [artistic convention] "The tibia creates a nice, flowing S-curve on the shin"; the lower leg can be built from "a thick column for the gastrocnemius and a thinner one for the soleus". — [Proko, How to Draw the Lower Leg (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-lower-leg-anatomy-for-artists/notes)
- [artistic convention] In side view "the front part of the leg can be quite straight and it creates a very nice contrast to the calf on the back"; the leg is "inherently asymmetric, creating a clear back and forth rhythm between the joints". — [Clip Studio Tips, drawing legs and feet](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/6714)

**Ankle**
- [anatomy] "The medial malleolus forms a broad prominence situated at a higher level and somewhat farther forward than the lateral malleolus." — [Wikipedia, Malleolus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malleolus)
- [anatomy] "Normally, the lateral malleolus is about a centimeter lower and posterior to the medial malleolus"; equal heights suggest ankle valgus. Inman's ankle axis passes 5 mm distal to the tip of the medial malleolus and 3 mm distal and 8 mm anterior to the tip of the lateral malleolus (so the joint axis slopes down toward the outside). — [Taylor & Francis Knowledge, Malleolus](https://taylorandfrancis.com/knowledge/Medicine_and_healthcare/Anatomy/Malleolus)

### Inferences
- **Front view centreline:** hip joints are set wide; the thigh axis runs down-and-in to the knee so the two knees are closer together than the hips; the shin then runs nearly vertical. A 5–7° angle between thigh and shin axes (apex inward at the knee) reproduces this; add 1–3° more for a female figure (wider pelvis, larger Q angle — but the sex difference is small and disputed). With thigh and shin axes meeting at a slight angle, the leg is a shallow zig-zag, not a straight pole.
- **Front-view contour rhythm (outside vs inside), top to bottom:** outer thigh fullest high (trochanter/vastus lateralis, then nearly flat); inner thigh fullest low (vastus medialis teardrop just above the kneecap); knee narrow and boxy; outer calf peak high; inner calf peak lower and heavier; inner ankle bone high and forward; outer ankle bone about 1 cm lower and further back. Each bulge on one side is opposite a flatter stretch on the other — the diagonal "high outside / low inside" alternation flips at the ankle.
- **Side-view contour:** front of thigh convex (rectus femoris bulging at mid-thigh), back of thigh flatter; knee slightly behind the hip–ankle line of gravity; shin front edge nearly straight; calf mass on the back peaking in the upper third to half of the lower leg and narrowing into the Achilles tendon by about 2/3 of the way down. This front-convex thigh over back-convex calf is the side-view S-curve.
- **Cross-sections:** thigh = rounded-front "bread slice" (wider at front, flatter behind/sides), not a circle; knee = squarish; calf = triangular/egg with the mass behind the bone; ankle = squarish, narrow.
- **Feet:** a toe-out of about 5–10° per foot is inside the normal gait range.

### Gaps
- No normative quiet-standing stance width (heel separation) was found; none of the retrieved sources state it.
- The femoral bicondylar/shaft obliquity angle as such (commonly quoted about 9–11° to the vertical) was not returned by any search; only the tibiofemoral 5–7° valgus was sourced.
- No source gave the height of the calf's widest point or the knee width as a fraction of segment length; "high on the lower leg" is qualitative.
- Patella size/placement and popliteal (back-of-knee) forms were not retrieved.
- Anatomy for Sculptors' leg-form pages (upper-leg forms, calf forms, male/female hip–thigh forms) exist but their content could not be opened. — [Anatomy for Sculptors project list](https://anatomy4sculptors.artstation.com/projects)

## 5. Neck and head

### Takeaway
The neck is a cylinder that leans forward and is cut obliquely: it is longer and attaches higher at the back (base of skull) than at the front (under the chin, ending at the pit of the neck between the collarbones), with the sternocleidomastoids running from behind each ear to that pit. The head is not a sphere but a cranial ball with flattened sides plus a boxy jaw hung from its front-lower part, carried forward of the trunk.

### Cited Findings
- [artistic convention] "The basic form of the neck is a cylinder. The axis of this cylinder is tilted forward to counterbalance the angle of rib cage"; "the neck is lower in the front and higher in the back." — [Proko, How to Draw Neck Muscles – Form (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-neck-muscles-form/notes)
- [artistic convention] "At the bottom of this cylinder, I imagine the collarbones, and at the top, the skull. This cylinder is longer at the back than at the front" (the hyoid marks the top at the front). — [Creative Bloq, how to draw a neck and shoulders](https://www.creativebloq.com/how-to/draw-neck-and-shoulders)
- [artistic convention] The sternocleidomastoids "insert into the mastoid process of the cranium and base of the cranium behind the ears" and split below, "attaching to the top of the collarbone and in the pit of the neck." — [Proko, How to Draw Neck Muscles – Anatomy and Motion (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-neck-muscles-anatomy-and-motion/notes)
- [artistic convention] Course notes: the neck "comes out of the torso and is never straight"; drawing the head upright makes it look "stuck on" — draw the head leaning forward from the torso. *(attribution per search extract)* — [Scribd course notes](https://www.scribd.com/document/450755377/course-notes-3)
- [measured] Ear (tragus) is forward of the C7 spinous process such that the C7→tragus line is about 48–50° above horizontal (women about 50°, men about 47.5°). — [Applied Sciences 2024 (MDPI)](https://mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/19/8639)
- [artistic convention] Loomis head: with the features removed there are "2 simple masses. A ball for the cranium and a boxy shape for the jaw"; the sides of the ball are sliced off to make flat side planes; the ear sits in the back lower quarter of the side plane; the face divides into thirds (hairline–brow, brow–nose base, nose base–chin); the head is about five eye-widths wide; the back of the cranium is wider than the brow. — [Proko, How to Draw the Head from Any Angle](https://proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-head-from-any-angle); [Fine Art Tutorials, Loomis method](https://finearttutorials.com/guide/loomis-method/); [21 Draw, Loomis method](https://www.21-draw.com/id/loomis-method/)
- [measured] Men's head and face dimensions exceed women's by 5–13 mm for head dimensions in the US Army ANSUR II survey (4,082 men, 1,986 women). *(attribution per search extract)* — [ANSUR II, Penn State OpenLab](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/)
- [measured, historical] Quetelet: the head goes 7.4 times into male stature and 7.2 times into female stature. — [Chest of Books, Human Body Drawing: Historical Aspects](https://chestofbooks.com/arts/drawing/Human-Body/Human-Body-Drawing-Historical-Aspects-Part-5.html)

### Inferences
- **Replace the ball head** with (a) a cranial ellipsoid whose longest axis is front-to-back, flattened at the sides, and (b) a jaw wedge attached to its lower front, so that in side view the back of the skull overhangs the neck and the chin projects forward and down.
- **Neck attachment:** the top of the neck cylinder should be an oblique cut — high at the back (meeting the skull at about ear-lobe / base-of-skull level) and low at the front (under the jaw, well below ear level). The bottom is oblique the same way: the pit of the neck in front is lower than the C7 bump behind (Proko's "lower in the front and higher in the back" applies to both ends), so the neck reads as a forward-leaning cylinder sliced by two roughly parallel sloping planes. The trapezius should fair the back of the neck out to the shoulders at the roughly 20° shoulder slope (section 2) rather than the neck meeting a flat shoulder deck at a right angle.
- The neck being a forward-leaning cylinder on a backward-leaning ribcage is the upper half of the side-view S (section 1).

### Gaps
- No measured head depth : height : width means were retrieved (the ANSUR query returned only the sex difference). Unverified background knowledge: adult head length (front–back) about 19–20 cm, breadth about 15 cm, chin-to-vertex height about 22–23 cm, i.e. roughly 7/8 as deep as tall and 2/3 as wide as tall.
- No source gave neck length, neck circumference/width relative to head, or the exact levels of the front and back neck attachments.
- Loomis's "Drawing the Head and Hands" itself was not opened; ratios such as "skull profile fits a square" are not sourced.

## 6. Proportion canons (Loomis, Richer, Bammes, Bridgman) and the fashion figure

### Takeaway
Real adults average about 7.5 heads tall (Richer's canon; Quetelet measured 7.4 men / 7.2 women); Loomis's "ideal" stretches this to 8 heads with landmarks falling on whole-head lines (nipples 2, navel 3, crotch 4 = mid-height, knees about 6, soles 8), and the fashion croquis stretches further to 9 heads by lengthening the legs while the torso stays about the same. In all canons the crotch/pubic bone, not the waist, is the vertical midpoint.

### Cited Findings
- [artistic convention] Loomis ideal figure (8 heads): head 1 = chin; "one head down to the nipples, another to the navel, and another to the crotch"; knees around the sixth head; feet at the eighth. Loomis's torso is very similar to Richer's; Loomis widens the shoulders to 2⅓ heads and keeps the hips at 1.5 heads. — [Proko, Human Proportions – Idealistic Figures (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-idealistic-figures/notes)
- [artistic convention / measured basis] Richer's canon is 7.5 heads and is treated as the "average" figure; "most real people are closer to 7.5 heads tall"; the average person "is generally 7-and-a-half heads tall". — [Proko, Human Proportions – Average Figure](https://www.proko.com/human-figure-proportions-average-richer); [Wikipedia, Body proportions](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Body_proportion)
- [artistic convention] Richer: female hips are 1.5 heads between the greater trochanters; male hips slightly narrower. — [Proko, Human Proportions – Average Figure (notes)](https://proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-average-figure/notes)
- [artistic convention] Tuts+ (Joumana Medlej) 8-head chart: knee joints on line 6 ("that line corresponds to the bottom of the kneecaps"); elbows on line 3; overall body width roughly 1.5 to 2 head-widths. *(body-width item: attribution per search extract)* — [Tuts+ Human Anatomy Fundamentals: Basic Body Proportions](https://design.tutsplus.com/articles/human-anatomy-fundamentals-basic-body-proportions--vector-18254)
- [artistic convention] "The halfway point of the total height falls at roughly the pubic bone, not the waist. This is one of the most common proportion mistakes in beginner figure drawing." — [Science Insights, proportioning a body](https://scienceinsights.org/how-to-proportion-a-body-for-figure-drawing/)
- [artistic convention] The male figure is widest at the shoulders, the female at the hips. — [Wikipedia, Body proportions](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Body_proportion)
- [fashion convention] 9-head croquis landmarks (tutorials differ slightly): bust at head 2 (one tutorial 2¼), waist at 3 (one 3¼), hips/crotch at 4 (range 4 to 4½), knees in head 6 (one 6½), mid-calf about 7½, ankles/ground at 9. Widths: shoulders about 1½ head-widths (about 2 for men), waist about ¾ to 1, hips about 1¼ (or equal to shoulders). — [Croqui Studio, fashion figure proportions](https://croqui-studio.pages.dev/blog/fashion-figure-proportions.html); [College Fashion, how to make fashion sketches](https://collegefashion.net/college-life/how-to-make-fashion-sketches/comment-page-3); [Vizcom, fashion croquis](https://vizcom.com/blog/fashion-croquis); [Science Insights, fashion model body](https://scienceinsights.org/how-to-draw-a-fashion-model-body-step-by-step/); [FashionSketch School (Scribd)](https://www.scribd.com/doc/267255980/FashionSketch-School)
- [measured, historical] Quetelet: head = 1/7.4 of stature in men, 1/7.2 in women. — [Chest of Books](https://chestofbooks.com/arts/drawing/Human-Body/Human-Body-Drawing-Historical-Aspects-Part-5.html)

### Inferences
- **Landmark heights as fractions of stature (8-head ideal, measured from the top):** chin 0.125, nipples 0.25, navel 0.375, crotch 0.50, knee (bottom of kneecap) 0.75, sole 1.0; elbow about 0.375 (navel/waist level), wrist about 0.50 (crotch level), fingertips about 0.60–0.63 (mid-thigh). For a realistic 7.5-head figure the head is 0.133 of stature and the same landmarks sit at nipples about 0.267, navel about 0.40, crotch about 0.53 (the Richer torso keeps the one-head spacing, so the legs are relatively shorter and the mid-height point falls just above the crotch — this last step is arithmetic from the cited spacing, not a quoted Richer value).
- **Fashion figure deviation:** torso landmarks stay on roughly the same head lines (2, 3, 4), but the crotch at 4 of 9 heads is at 0.44 of stature instead of 0.50, so legs are about 56% of height instead of 50%; the head is 0.111 of stature; shoulders, waist and hips are all narrower in head-widths (1.5 / 0.75–1 / 1.25) than the artistic male figure (2⅓ / — / 1.5). A fashion mannequin mode can therefore be produced mostly by scaling leg length (especially below the knee: knee at 6–6.5, ground at 9) and shrinking head size, leaving torso forms unchanged.
- For a realistic default the mannequin should use about 7.5 heads; 8 heads is an acceptable "ideal/heroic" setting.

### Gaps
- **Bammes ("Die Gestalt des Menschen") and Bridgman proportion systems were not retrievable** — searches returned no usable description of either; nothing is reported about them beyond Bridgman's block shorthand in section 4.
- **Loomis's female plate** ("Ideal Proportion, Female") values were not retrieved; the book lists the plate but no source gave its numbers. Unverified background knowledge: Loomis draws the ideal female also on an 8-head scale, about 2 head-widths at the shoulders, with the same head-line landmarks.
- Goldfinger, Richer's own text and Zarins's proportion pages were not opened.
- Sources disagree on Richer's shoulder width (an extract mentioned "about 1⅔ head heights" ambiguously); not used.

## 7. Rules of thumb: which few forms make a simplified figure read as human

### Takeaway
Teaching sources agree that believability comes from large relationships before detail: opposed tilts of head, ribcage and pelvis in side view; correct landmark heights; two distinct torso masses with a waist between; tapering, asymmetric limbs with offset bulges; and limbs that are not oversized at calf and forearm. Detail (muscle definition) matters far less than these.

### Cited Findings
- [artistic convention] Proko's simplification sequence is gesture → bean (ribcage and pelvis masses with tilt/twist) → robo bean (boxes) → landmarks → mannequinization (torso as two boxes, neck a cylinder, limbs as cylinders and boxes). — [Proko, Mannequinization](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/mannequinization-structure-of-the-human-body); [Proko, The Bean](https://www.proko.com/the-bean/)
- [artistic convention] Bony landmarks "give us clues to the gesture and points from which to measure the proportions" — e.g. the acromion shows the shoulder angle, the greater trochanter marks the side of the hip. — [Proko, Landmarks of the Human Body (notes)](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/landmarks-of-the-human-body/notes)
- [artistic convention] The ribcage and pelvis tilt in opposition and the head is held forward by the neck; these three masses, not the limbs, set the pose. — [Michael Hampton (archive.org text)](https://archive.org/download/michael-hampton-figure-drawing-design-an/Michael_Hampton_Figure_Drawing_Design_an_text.pdf); [Line of Action, gesture basics: head, ribcage and pelvis](https://line-of-action.com/zh/article/gesture-basics-2-torso-and-hips)
- [artistic convention] Limbs: each segment tapers, with "an undulating rhythm of swelling and narrowing"; the leg is "inherently asymmetric"; Bridgman alternates cross-section character (round thigh, square knee, triangular calf, square ankle). — [Rubenstein, Drawing the Leg](https://ephraimrubenstein.com/wp-content/uploads/DrawingLegRotOCR.pdf); [Clip Studio Tips](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/6714); [Creative Bloq, how to draw legs](https://www.creativebloq.com/how-to/draw-legs)
- [3D modelling advice] Lock proportions before adding detail (beginners add pores and folds "before locking down the core proportions"); use the head as the measuring unit; build the base mesh at real-world scale; reviewers of beginner meshes single out over-thick calves and forearms. — [MAGES, common mistakes in 3D character design](https://mages.edu.sg/blog/common-mistakes-in-3d-character-design-how-to-avoid-them/); [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2618768); [Blender Studio, realistic character workflow](https://studio.blender.org/training/realistic-human-research/chapter/design-blocking-sculpting); [Blender Artists thread](https://blenderartists.org/t/anatomy-proportions-need-someone-to-check-dummy-model-for-errors/675887)

### Inferences
Priority list for the procedural mannequin, ordered by how much "wooden doll" each removes (synthesised from sections 1–6):
1. **Side-view S:** ribcage leaning back, pelvis tipped forward about 10°, lumbar hollow about 4 cm, neck leaning forward with the ear over the shoulder and well ahead of the neck base, knees very slightly behind the hip–ankle line. A straight vertical stack is the single biggest give-away.
2. **Two torso masses and a waist:** egg ribcage (narrow top, widest low) + pelvis block + narrower waist bridge; sloping (about 20°) shoulder line instead of a flat deck; front-to-back depth about half the width (1 head deep vs 2 heads wide at the shoulders).
3. **Shoulder cap and armpit:** deltoid mass overhanging the top of the arm and ending mid-upper-arm; front and rear armpit webs so arms grow out of the chest and back instead of being pinned on.
4. **Non-circular, twisting limb sections:** upper arm deeper than wide, forearm wide then flat at the wrist; thigh bread-slice, knee square, calf mass behind, ankle square.
5. **Offset bulges:** outer-high/inner-low at thigh and calf, reversed at the ankle bones; calf peak high on the back of the lower leg with a straight shin in front.
6. **Converging legs:** hips wide, knees closer, shins near-vertical (5–7° thigh–shin angle).
7. **Head and neck:** ellipsoid cranium + jaw wedge; neck cut obliquely, higher at the back; trapezius fairing neck into shoulders.
8. **Sex dimorphism by a few parameters only:** shoulder breadth (40 vs 36 cm bony), shoulder slope (21° vs 19°), pelvis wider and shorter in women with ribcage narrower than pelvis, pelvic tilt (about 9° vs 12°), carrying angle (about 10° vs 13–15°), Q angle/knee convergence (+2–3° in women), bust mass on ribs 2–6, softer hip contour.
9. **Rest pose:** elbows bent about 15°, palms toward thighs, fingertips at mid-thigh, feet slightly toed-out.
